## Scope and purpose

Cover personal account data, workspace business records, explicit Brand sharing, onboarding answers/documents, automation consent/drafts, both notification outboxes, object storage, runtime copies and recovery backups. Existing delete buttons, withdrawal, unpublishing, suspension and CSV exports remain separate operations with their current contracts.

Provide three distinct request scopes:

| Scope | Approved requester and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Personal account | Verified account holder, with recent password/MFA verification. Includes their identity, security-event projection, memberships and personal automation preferences/history. It never grants another person's private drafts or every record in a workspace they belong to. |
| Whole workspace | Current verified owner with fresh MFA; export/delete only this workspace's authorized data. Other members' private drafts and account/security records are excluded from an owner download and separately inventoried for deletion. Owners retain current finance permissions. |
| External person's data | Offline, identity-verified request reviewed by the designated privacy owner and affected workspace owner. Match and review exact record IDs; an email string alone cannot authorize a cross-workspace search, export or deletion. No public email-lookup endpoint. |

**Confirmed product boundary (D40):** tenant members/admins cannot erase a whole workspace. Platform grants and approved support access do not grant tenant-private export access. Offline reviewers see minimized request metadata; access to content needs separately scoped authority. Last-owner accounts must transfer ownership or complete a separately approved workspace closure. Platform-owner accounts remain protected until a separately audited grant transfer/revocation preserves another usable owner.

## Export behavior

- Authorize at request, build and download using current membership, account status, MFA and scope version. A revoked requester cannot download a previously generated archive. Provide a verified offline path for people who can no longer sign in; do not weaken normal suspension checks.
- Inventory all eligible records without the screen's 100-row limits or current list filters. Build a consistent authorized snapshot; no deferred query may run after tenant context ends. Large exports use bounded chunks with a snapshot/version strategy and fail/restart if consistency cannot be established.
- Include only allowlisted business/account fields. Exclude password/reset/session/token hashes, raw link codes, encrypted mail payloads, MFA secrets/recovery codes, private object keys, other people's private drafts and platform audit/support internals. An account export may include that account's own private drafts; workspace ownership alone is insufficient. CSV keeps the existing formula-injection protections.
- A Brand export contains only currently authorized shared projections, never the Partner's private CRM. A Partner export includes its own sharing history. Ended/withdrawn sharing cannot regain visibility through an archive download; recheck access or invalidate/rebuild the archive when the included scope changes. Copies already downloaded by a recipient cannot be recalled; record this limitation.
- Only clean, checksum-verified document bytes enter an ordinary export. Quarantined, rejected and staging-only unscanned documents are listed as excluded with a reason. A verified offline disclosure of excluded bytes needs a separate approved handling procedure; never release them through the normal download path.
- Archive manifest records schema version, generation time, request scope, included categories/counts/checksums, exclusions and retained exceptions. Minimize filenames and logs. Store archives privately with an expiry ledger and retryable removal, including abandoned partial archives. Downloads use attachment/no-store headers and record metadata-only audit.

## Deletion lifecycle and concurrency

**Proposed states:** `requested → review → blocked | cooling_off → erasing → completed | completed_with_retained_records`; cancellation is allowed before erasing. Per-step failures leave the request in `erasing` with a retryable failure record. `completed_with_retained_records` requires an approved exception list and next review date; it is never presented as complete erasure. Request metadata carries scope, requester, identity-verification method/time, policy version, expected record versions, manifest fingerprint and accountable reviewer. Never store submitted identity documents by default.

1. **Review:** compute exact dependent rows and storage references, including other workspaces' shared copies, ownership/finance/audit blockers and every queued action. Keep the manifest private; changing the scope or protected dependencies invalidates its approval. Do not infer consent to close other workspaces from an account request.
2. **Confirm:** require the shared `confirmAction`/`ConfirmationDialog`, affected account/workspace name, consequences, explicit action label and initially focused Cancel. Retain required password/MFA/reason/version checks, errors and duplicate-submit guards. Keep the dialog outside existing forms/drawers. Recheck authority and scope on the server.
3. **Restrict atomically:** establish the deletion barrier, revoke relevant sessions/capabilities/invitations/support/sharing/publication, disable schedules and invalidate approvals. Block new imports, uploads, invitations, exports and workflow reconstruction for that scope. Personal closure must affect only that person's authority/work unless a separate workspace closure is approved. Record content-free audit in the same transaction.
4. **Resolve in-flight work:** use a reviewed lock order compatible with membership, partnership, lead, consent, finance and outbox locks. Test both lock orders against SMTP admission and file access. A send admitted before the barrier may finish; display it as already handed off or uncertain. Never claim recall. A later reservation is refused; cancellation never refunds mail-budget consumption or authorizes retry of uncertainty. Cover account/security/Brand-outcome mail as well as business mail.
5. **Erase eligible content:** after the approved window and fresh dependency check, remove private records, snapshots, recipient fields and duplicate shared copies according to approved class rules. Withdrawn rows can still contain copied content; loss of visibility alone is insufficient. Finance/audit/consent exceptions require explicit restricted retention. Use reviewed schema/functions to reconcile immutable rows and user/workspace foreign keys; no browser migration credentials, blanket cascade, disabled RLS or unrestricted bypass.
6. **Remove storage durably:** commit a private deletion ledger before deleting metadata needed to locate bytes. Retry exact object/version removals idempotently; verify absence and provider failures. File errors prevent completion. Account for active references, submission snapshots, exports, quarantine and separately inventoried transfer/backup copies. A shared bucket prefix is not sufficient authority for bulk deletion.
7. **Finish and preserve recovery safety:** clear relevant cache/session/failed-job payload copies, request provider-side actions where supported and retain minimal request/exception/erasure metadata. Keep deletions enforceable after Redis loss and database restore. Completion requires verified row/object disposition and recorded provider/backup limitations. Notify through an approved channel only when delivery remains authorized.

## Retention and recovery rules

Each data class needs purpose, retention trigger/duration, hold reason, visibility, erasure method and evidence. A hold applies to exact necessary records and can be reviewed/released; it does not justify retaining an entire workspace's contact details. D32's audit-preservation rule remains in force: any later anonymization or pruning needs an explicit policy change and compatible schema migration.

Immutable submissions, consent events, financial idempotency receipts and audit foreign keys require deliberate treatment. Pseudonymized references are still retained data; do not report them as erased. Keep content out of the erasure register, but protect and retain enough routing information to prevent resurrection. Define its retention with the maximum recoverable backup age and replay requirements before purging it.

No claim that an application key rotation selectively deletes data: the same key protects unrelated secrets and recovery assets. Backup expiration must cover off-host copies, keys and any object versions, with verification. A restore starts isolated with workers/mail/public routes disabled, applies approved erasures/holds, reconciles objects and verifies access before release. Existing encrypted restore evidence does not prove this new behavior.

## Delivered PRIV-02 export interface

Implemented locally on the working tree based on `3ec3847`. Entry points are **Privacy exports** in the shared account menu and **Download your data** in workspace account security. Uses FoundationShell, ContentSection, FormField, SelectField, ActionGroup, StatusBadge and the shared Button; ExportHistory contains the receipt list.

| Route | Delivered behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /account/exports` | Current account's latest 20 receipts and available owned workspaces; no platform/support override |
| `POST /account/exports` | Scope `account` or `workspace`, workspace UUID where selected, current password, fresh single-use TOTP when enrolled; workspace scope always requires enrollment and current ownership, including when staging enforcement is off |
| `POST /account/exports/verify` | Reverify password/TOTP for downloads; session proof lasts 15 minutes and is invalidated by changed password/email/verification/authenticator basis |
| `GET /account/exports/{export}/download` | Requester-only authenticated ZIP; rechecks current authorization, snapshot fingerprint, expiry, size and archive checksum before materializing bytes inside the tenant transaction |

`privacy_exports` and append-only runtime `privacy_export_events` force RLS. Scope/lifetime are immutable; runtime cannot invent future creation dates or mark cleanup finished. A committed building receipt precedes file creation, covering interrupted/rolled-back builds. Both final and partial ZIP names derive only from the receipt UUID, under the private local `privacy-exports` disk (0700 directory / 0600 file). Archive storage must be shared by app and scheduler on the selected topology; existing Compose's private-storage volume supplies that locally. A rollout must apply migration `2026_11_20_000001_create_privacy_exports`, refresh runtime grants and run matching application/scheduler code. Rollback succeeds only before any export receipt exists; afterwards preserve the ledger and fix forward. No shared/staging migration or deployment occurred.

Account archives include allowlisted identity, current membership metadata, security events, the applicant's Brand requests and their own currently authorized automation preferences/schedules/runs/consent/drafts/notification metadata. Workspace archives include own CRM/templates/public-presence/onboarding/safe documents, owner finance/plan metadata, redacted tenant audit, invitation/join-link metadata and currently visible relationship/shared-lead/profile projections. Other members' private automation and account/security data remain excluded; personal export supplies the requester's private drafts. Original fields/hashes/secrets not on the explicit projections are never selected. Manifest exclusions describe absent provider/backup/internal copies; this is not a complete external-person disclosure workflow.

Generation and download use a restricted-role **repeatable-read snapshot**, membership locks and partnership-before-registration sharing locks. A withdrawal that wins the lock first refuses an older snapshot; one admitted after export materialization waits for that transaction. A later included-data change invalidates the archive; the UI asks for a fresh export. Raw document keys stay outside the archive; ordinary downloads include only clean, checksum-verified originals under UUID filenames. Unsafe/unscanned documents are listed as exclusions.

This first implementation generates synchronously in bounded keyset chunks, with **10,000 rows**, **8 MiB projected row data**, and **48 MiB archive payload** (plus up to 1 MiB ZIP/manifest overhead) as safety limits. Oversize requests fail explicitly with an assisted-export message, never a silently truncated archive. ZIP uses built-in PharData without adding a dependency. CSV retains formula-injection protection; manifest lists category counts and file hashes. Creation is throttled to three attempts/hour, verification and downloads to ten/minute, with the shared failed-credential guard. Password/code form values and defaults clear after submission.

Downloads stop at 24 hours from request creation. `privacy:purge-exports` runs every five minutes, up to 500 eligible receipts per pass, and also reconciles failed or abandoned (15-minute) builds. It skips active build/download locks, verifies exact final/partial file absence before marking expired, retries failures and logs only aggregate failure counts. Cleanup delays/outages may retain inaccessible files past expiry; external retention and multi-host topology remain operational gates. These are export-artifact removals only, not account deletion, document-orphan reconciliation or ADM-07 cleanup execution.


## Delivered PRIV-03 closure interface

Implemented on `feat/privacy-closure`, based on export commit `64b1a6f`. **Confirmed:** this implements D40 access restriction, review and cancellation. It neither erases business records nor schedules automatic erasure. `not_before` is a minimum 30-day boundary, not a promise of deletion on that date. The only implemented states are `cooling_off` and `cancelled`; later lifecycle states above remain proposals.

| Route | Delivered behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /account/closure` | Own account impact preview; `scope=workspace&org_id=<UUID>` for a currently owned workspace; latest 100 own/owned request receipts. Private record locators and manifest hashes are not exposed to handlers. |
| `POST /account/closure` | Scope, workspace where applicable, current preview hash, reason, password and fresh single-use authenticator code when enrolled; workspace closure always requires enrollment. Shared modal confirmation identifies the subject and immediate access loss. |
| `POST /account/closure/{closure}/cancel` | Fresh password/code, reason and request version. Workspace cancellation requires an enrolled owner and at least one usable verified, enrolled owner. |
| `GET /admin/privacy-requests` | `platform.owner` only, after administrator MFA; paginated minimized request identity, reason, category counts, timing and accountable handler. No tenant-content or private manifest access. |
| `POST /admin/privacy-requests/{closure}` | Fresh operator password/code and request version; assign to self, record review after assignment, or cancel with a verified subject-request reference. Review does not approve erasure. |

Closure is blocked for accounts with active platform grants. A last usable workspace owner must transfer ownership or separately close that workspace first. Account closure signs out existing sessions, clears reset/remember credentials, revokes the person's exports/support access and cancels their eligible pending automation/mail across workspaces. It does not close those workspaces or remove other members' work. Future sign-in opens only closure/authenticator/security routes, with normal administrative suspension checks still enforced.

Workspace closure denies all members new workspace work and denies public/shared/capability access, including existing onboarding sessions and return links. It unpublishes media kits, revokes invitations, join/lead links, partnerships and support grants, disables schedules/preferences, cancels ready runs/private drafts/pending outboxes and revokes workspace exports. Finance, audit, documents and history are retained. Cancellation restores current eligible interactive access only: ended capabilities, partnerships, publications, exports and cancelled work remain ended. An existing suspension is not cleared.

`privacy_closure_requests` and `privacy_closure_events` force RLS and have no runtime table grants or policies. Fixed-search-path functions own the narrowly scoped operations. The private review inventory is capped at 20,000 records and records locators/content fingerprints, not copied record bodies. A changed fingerprint requires a fresh preview. Session rotation is excluded from the approval fingerprint; password/MFA secrets and counters are excluded from fingerprints. Submitted reasons/case references must avoid identity documents or private content.

Shared account/workspace advisory barriers precede membership and subject locks. Restriction/cancellation takes exclusive scope barriers; owner transitions serialize separately. Repeatable-read export transactions also lock user/workspace anchors to refuse stale snapshots after a closer commits. Partnership locks precede shared-registration locks. Ordinary work admitted first may finish before closure; work admitted after closure is denied. Mail already reserved may finish or remain uncertain; no recall, budget refund or automatic resend is claimed. Both durable outboxes and direct verification/reset mail participate in these guards.

Rollout requires `2026_11_20_000002_create_privacy_closures`, refreshed runtime grants and matching app/worker/scheduler code. Deploy with old workers stopped/drained before migration and restart all processes on the matching code: an old process lacks the application barriers. Rollback refuses once any closure history exists; preserve the ledger and fix forward. Existing archives revoked by closure are discovered by the export purge for exact-file removal. No staging migration or deployment occurred. See [closure verification and remaining gates](closure-evidence.md).
